2026 South Carolina Recreational Season
 Thanks to the nearly 3,000 South Carolina anglers that got their red snapper experimental permits! The October season has concluded, but we’re asking anglers to continue declaring and reporting bottom fishing trips on the VESL platform through the end of November. Both in season and out of season data is critical and we need your help in working toward the goal of more state-based management. NOAA fisheries will evaluate the results of this regional effort and determine if additional days will be provided in December. Please stay tuned for updates.
 About
 The South Carolina Red Snapper Project is a state-based angler-driven effort to collect better data on red snapper fishing. This is an experiment that relies on cooperation between SCDNR and anglers to document catch and properly manage this important fishery.
 Step 1
 Obtain a free red snapper experimental permit on GoOutdoorsSC on or after October 2nd. Anglers must first have a valid SC saltwater fishing license or privilege.
 Step 2
 After obtaining your permit on GoOutdoorsSC, you will receive an email containing instructions for how to download the free VESL app and create your account.
 Step 3
 Planning to keep red snapper? Declare your trip in the VESL app. You may declare up to 5 days before a trip. EACH angler planning to keep a red snapper must declare a trip and report their catch.
 Step 4
 Report your catch on the VESL app within 24 hours of completion. Make sure to hit the "COMPLETE" button after reporting your catch. Once an angler has reported for one trip, they may declare additional trips.
 Why Reporting Matters
 Your data provides real-time catch and trip information to fisheries managers and reduces the data uncertainty that contributed to short fishing seasons in the past. This exempted fishing permit is an experiment where anglers directly contribute data that helps managers maintain a healthy fishery. South Carolina’s ability to continue this experiment and provide greater fishing access in the following years relies on the cooperation of our anglers.

 Descending device rigged and ready with at least 60 feet of line and 16+ oz of weight
 Additionally, anglers fishing under the exempted fishing permit are required to USE their descending device when releasing red snapper that show signs of barotrauma
 Non-offset, non stainless steel circle hooks 
 Dehooking tool

 FAQ's
 What is the purpose of South Carolina’s exempted fishing permit (EFP)?
 An exempted fishing permit allows the South Carolina Department of Natural Resources to authorize fishing activity that would otherwise be prohibited for specific purposes; in this case, the EFP is to collect data on recreational red snapper fishing. This EFP was approved to better understand the number of recreational red snapper anglers, their fishing effort, and the number of fish, both kept and released. This information has been difficult to obtain accurately under the current data collection system. The red snapper project is an experiment and the success of that experiment and its ability to continue in the future will rely on the cooperation of our anglers. 
 Why is SCDNR Law Enforcement checking my boat each time I go fishing for red snapper under the Experimental Fishing Permit (EFP)?
 Law enforcement checks are an important part of the Red Snapper Experimental Fishing Permit (EFP) program. SCDNR is responsible for ensuring that participants comply with the EFP terms and conditions and that information collected during the project is accurate and reliable.
Each fishing trip is a separate activity under the EFP; being checked on a previous trip does not exempt a participant from being checked again. You may therefore encounter SCDNR Law Enforcement officers on multiple trips during the project. Participants should keep all required permits, trip information, and licenses readily available while fishing and when returning to the landing.
